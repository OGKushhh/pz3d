# City Lore — 3 Concept Options

Three fictional city concepts for the alpha map. Each is engineered to fit the locked biomes (Suburbia, Parks, Farmland, Forest, Commercial Strip, Industrial, River/Wetlands, Subway, Downtown, Military Zone) and the locked design principles (fog, non-trivial roads, water as obstacle, landmarks far apart).

Pick one, or remix elements from each.

---

## Option A — Halberd Bay, OR-equivalent

**Real-world analog:** Pacific Northwest coastal town (Washington / Oregon coast).

**Geographic premise:** A small coastal city built around a deep-water bay. Founded as a naval shipyard town in the 1920s. Boomed during WWII. Declined when the naval base closed in the 1980s. Reinvented itself as a tech-forward "second-tier" port city by the 2000s.

**Map fit (4 km × 3 km):**
- **West edge:** Pacific Ocean (the bay itself — half-flooded docks, beached container ships)
- **East edge:** Coastal mountain range (Forests, Farmland in the foothills)
- **North edge:** River delta flowing into the bay (River & Wetlands biome)
- **South edge:** Highway 101-equivalent (the only road in or out — Military Zone gates it)
- **Center:** Downtown + Commercial Strip + Subway + Industrial Park
- **Surrounding:** Suburbia rings the city; Parks & Greenways along the river; Forest on the eastern slopes

**Lore hooks:**
- Outbreak started at the naval base (Military Zone). Officially sealed "for quarantine".
- The bay has a permanent fog bank — weather identity for Industrial + River biomes.
- The coastal highway is the only land route out. Blocked by Military Zone checkpoints.
- A ship ran aground in the bay during the evacuation — accessible by boat, has rare loot.
- Underground subway connected to a Cold War-era civil defense bunker (now the Subway biome).

**Vibe:** Grey, rainy, foggy, naval, slightly haunted. Pacific Northwest gothic. Twin Peaks meets The Last of Us.

**Naming conventions:**
- Streets named after WWII naval officers (Nimitz Ave, Halsey Blvd)
- Districts named after shipyards (Pier 7, North Wharf, Bremerton Annex)
- Local landmark: "The Breakwater" — a 1920s naval jetty that's now the iconic visual landmark, visible from anywhere on the west coast

**Why it works for our biomes:**
- Industrial = old shipyards + container port (organic, fits lore)
- River & Wetlands = bay delta (perfect)
- Subway = Cold War bunker under downtown (fits naval history)
- Military Zone = the closed naval base (perfect)
- Forest = coastal mountain range (organic)

## Option B — Carthage, Gulf Coast

**Real-world analog:** Gulf Coast port city (Mobile, AL / Biloxi, MS / Galveston, TX).

**Geographic premise:** A Southern port city on the Gulf of Mexico. Founded by French colonists in the 1700s. Cotton port → oil refinery town → modern petrochemical hub. Hot, humid, hurricane-prone, racially and culturally layered.

**Map fit (4 km × 3 km):**
- **South edge:** Gulf of Mexico (Commercial Strip along the beachfront, boardwalk, pier)
- **North edge:** Forested swamp (Forest biome + River & Wetlands merge)
- **East edge:** A river delta (River & Wetlands biome)
- **West edge:** Highway out (Military Zone gates it — old Air Force base)
- **Center:** Downtown + Subway + Commercial Strip + Industrial (oil refinery)
- **Surrounding:** Suburbia (old plantation houses turned residential), Farmland (former cotton fields), Parks & Greenways

**Lore hooks:**
- Outbreak started at a petrochemical plant explosion (Industrial Park). Initial cover-up.
- Hurricane during the outbreak caused the levee to break — flooded parts of the city (River & Wetlands).
- The old Air Force base (Military Zone) was used as a FEMA evacuation center — overrun.
- Bayou legends: locals say the swamp has been "wrong" since the 1800s. Now it's worse.
- A long bridge to a barrier island (Commercial Strip on the beach) — the only thing connecting it to the mainland.

**Vibe:** Hot, humid, Southern gothic, petrochemical, decaying grandeur. True Blood meets The Last of Us.

**Naming conventions:**
- Streets named after Confederate/French figures (Beauregard St, Bienville Ave)
- Districts named after plantations (Magnolia Acres, Belle Chase)
- Local landmark: "The Refinery" — the petrochemical plant, visible from anywhere in the city, burning its eternal pilot light

**Why it works for our biomes:**
- Industrial = petrochemical refinery (organic, fits)
- River & Wetlands = bayou (perfect)
- Subway = unusual for Southern cities; explain as "abandoned subway from a 1970s urban renewal project that never finished"
- Military Zone = old Air Force base (perfect)
- Forest = the swamp inland (organic)

## Option C — Vance, Appalachian Valley

**Real-world analog:** Appalachian mountain town (Wheeling, WV / Johnson City, TN).

**Geographic premise:** A mountain town built in a narrow valley, bisected by a river. Founded in the 1800s as a mining town. Boomed during the steel age. Declined when the mines closed. Now a small regional hub with a state college.

**Map fit (4 km × 3 km):**
- **North edge:** Mountain ridge (Forest biome, impassable — forces east-west travel)
- **South edge:** Mountain ridge (Forest biome, impassable)
- **East edge:** River exits the valley through a narrow gap (Military Zone gates the gap — old mining tunnel)
- **West edge:** River enters the valley through another gap (Farmland)
- **Center:** Downtown + Subway + Commercial Strip + Industrial (old steel mill)
- **Surrounding:** Suburbia on the hillsides, Farmland upstream, Parks & Greenways along the river

**Lore hooks:**
- Outbreak started at the mining compound up the river. Toxic leak from a sealed mine.
- The valley only has two road exits — both blocked by the Military Zone.
- The river is the only "free" route in or out — but you need a boat.
- Old coal mine tunnels under downtown = the Subway biome.
- Local folklore: "the hills have eyes" — old mountain myth now made real.
- The state college was the evacuation center — overrun.

**Vibe:** Isolated, claustrophobic, mountain, mining heritage, deeply rural. Winter's Bone meets The Descent.

**Naming conventions:**
- Streets named after miners and founders (Hargrove St, Vance Blvd)
- Districts named after coal seams (Black Vein, Slate Run)
- Local landmark: "The Trestle" — an abandoned coal-mine rail trestle over the river, visible from anywhere in the valley

**Why it works for our biomes:**
- Industrial = old steel mill (organic, fits)
- River & Wetlands = the river through the valley (works, but smaller scale)
- Subway = old coal mines (perfect — explorable mines, no actual subway needed)
- Military Zone = gates the valley exits (organic)
- Forest = mountains all around (perfect)
- Farmland = upstream valley farmland (organic)

## My recommendation: **Option A — Halberd Bay**

Reasons:
1. **Fog identity** — your locked design principle ("draw distance fog") gets a lore reason: the bay has a permanent fog bank. Every screenshot justifies the fog.
2. **Bay as central water feature** — gives the River & Wetlands biome a large, visible landmark ("the bay is that way"). Players always know which direction is west.
3. **Naval shipyard for Industrial** — organic excuse for warehouses, cranes, loading docks, rail yards, shipping containers. All the Industrial POIs are just... a shipyard.
4. **Cold War bunker for Subway** — gives the subway a unique architectural identity (not just generic tunnels).
5. **Closed naval base for Military Zone** — the outbreak origin story is built in. "Don't go to the base. They sealed it for a reason."
6. **Visual coherence with MoGen native look** — the stylized low-poly aesthetic loves overcast, foggy, low-saturation environments. Halberd Bay is *that*.

Option B is great but harder to render stylized (Southern heat needs saturated colors, which fights MoGen-native restraint). Option C is excellent but the linear river limits water-as-obstacle play.
